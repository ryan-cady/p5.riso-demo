Paper & Print Prep

What to know about paper stock, how it affects your print, and what to hand off to the printer so the job runs smoothly.

How Paper Affects the Print

RISO ink is oil-based and semi-transparent. The paper you print on changes the result significantly — more than in most other printing processes.

  • Uncoated / bond paper — the most common RISO stock. Ink absorbs into the fibers, producing a slightly matte, slightly textured result. Colors appear slightly muted compared to screen. Most RISO printers stock a mid-weight uncoated paper (60–80 lb text).
  • Newsprint — very absorbent, ink spreads and feathers slightly. Colors look warmer and more saturated due to the paper's yellowish tone. Great for a zine or newspaper aesthetic but less precise for fine detail.
  • Colored paper — the paper color tints everything, including "unprinted" areas. Printing fluorescent pink on cream paper looks different than on white. Factor the paper color into your design; it effectively acts as a third ink.
  • Heavier stock (100+ lb) — less show-through from back-to-back printing, but may not feed reliably in all machines. Check with the print shop before designing for heavy stock.
  • Coated paper — ink sits on the surface rather than absorbing, which can result in smearing or slow dry times. Most RISO shops avoid it. Ask first.

Standard Print Sizes

RISO machines print up to a maximum paper size (usually A3 or 11×17"). Common finished sizes: 

// At 150 DPI — multiply inches by 150 for canvas size:
// Half-letter:  5.5" × 8.5"   →  createCanvas(825, 1275)
// Letter:       8.5" × 11"    →  createCanvas(1275, 1650)
// A5:           5.8" × 8.3"   →  createCanvas(870, 1245)
// A4:           8.3" × 11.7"  →  createCanvas(1245, 1755)
// Tabloid:      11"  × 17"    →  createCanvas(1650, 2550)
// A3:           11.7" × 16.5" →  createCanvas(1755, 2475)

Half-letter (5.5×8.5") is the most common size for class projects — it's easy to handle, fits standard paper, and prints quickly. Larger sizes cost more and take longer to set up.

Bleed

If your design goes to the edge of the paper, add a bleed — artwork that extends past the trim line so there's no white edge if the cut is slightly off. Standard bleed is 0.125" on each side: 

// Half-letter with 0.125" bleed on all sides:
// Width:  5.5 + 0.25 = 5.75"  →  5.75 × 150 = 863px
// Height: 8.5 + 0.25 = 8.75"  →  8.75 × 150 = 1313px
createCanvas(863, 1313);
pixelDensity(1);

Note that most RISO printing is not trimmed — you design to the paper edge and what you see is what you get. Bleed only matters if the print shop is cutting the sheets after printing.

What Fluorescent Inks Look Like on Paper

Fluorescent inks (fluorescent pink, fluorescent lime, fluorescent orange) look dramatically different in print than on screen. On screen they appear bright but fairly normal. On paper they glow — the fluorescent pigment reacts to ambient light in a way no display can replicate.

This means: don't judge fluorescent ink colors by the screen preview. They will be more vivid and saturated in print, especially in well-lit or naturally lit environments.

Pre-Submission Checklist

Before sending files to the printer, verify:

  • One grayscale PNG per ink layer — e.g. fluorescentpink_0.png, blue_1.png
  • A composite PNG as a reference for how layers should align
  • Canvas dimensions match your intended print size at 150 DPI
  • Files are grayscale — no color tint on the layer PNGs
  • Each file is clearly named with the ink color it represents
  • You've noted which layer prints first (if it matters to your design)
  • You've noted the paper size, orientation (portrait / landscape), and any paper stock preference
  • Bleed is included if the design goes to the edge
  • Any important notes about registration or alignment are written out

Talking to the Printer

When you hand off files, include a short note with:

  • Paper size and orientation — "half-letter, portrait"
  • Ink colors by layer — "layer 0 = fluorescent pink, layer 1 = blue"
  • Print order — which color goes down first (if you have a preference)
  • Paper stock — if you have a preference, say so; otherwise the shop will use their default
  • Quantity — how many copies

The more clearly you communicate, the fewer surprises you'll get back from the press.
